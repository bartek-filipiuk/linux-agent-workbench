Three things worth knowing first

  1. Your hand is a sky ruler

    You don’t need equipment to start. An outstretched pinky finger spans roughly one degree of sky, three middle fingers together span about five degrees, and a closed fist spans about ten — handy for estimating how far apart two stars or planets appear.

    Source: NASA Science, Skywatching FAQ

  2. Look along the terminator

    The Moon shows the most texture away from full phase, along the terminator — the line between lunar day and night — where low-angle sunlight throws long shadows across craters and mountain ranges, making relief far easier to see.

    Source: NASA Science, Moon Viewing Tips

  3. Protect your night vision

    Full dark adaptation takes time, and bright white light can quickly disrupt it. Use a red flashlight or headlamp to read maps or find your footing, and give your eyes a while to adjust before expecting to see faint stars or the Milky Way.

    Source: National Park Service, Where to Stargaze

Five principles of responsible outdoor lighting

The night sky isn’t only lost at dark-sky parks — it’s shaped by every porch light and floodlight left on nearby. These five principles, paraphrased from DarkSky International and the Illuminating Engineering Society, describe how outdoor lighting can stay useful without washing out the stars.

Useful
Only install a light if it serves a clear purpose, and weigh how it affects people, wildlife, and the surrounding dark.
Targeted
Shield fixtures and aim them downward so light lands on what it needs to, not on neighboring yards or the sky.
Low level
Use the least amount of light that does the job — bright, reflective surfaces need even less than you’d expect.
Controlled
Let motion sensors, timers, and dimmers turn lights off or down when nothing needs them on.
Warm-colored
Favor warm-spectrum bulbs over harsh blue-white light, which scatters more and disrupts nocturnal life more.

Source: DarkSky International & Illuminating Engineering Society, Five Principles for Responsible Outdoor Lighting
